# 📊 Data Visualization

## 📌 Project Overview

This project was completed as part of the **CodAlpha Data Analytics Internship**.

The objective of this project is to analyze an Online Retail dataset and create meaningful visualizations to understand sales performance, revenue trends, product demand, and business patterns.

Using Python visualization libraries, raw transaction data was transformed into charts that help communicate business insights and support data-driven decision-making.

## 🎯 Project Objectives

- Analyze sales and revenue trends.
- Identify top-performing countries.
- Understand product performance by revenue and quantity.
- Compare weekday and weekend sales.
- Examine monthly and daily revenue patterns.
- Explore revenue distribution.
- Identify relationships between numerical variables.
- Present business findings through visualizations.

## 📈 Visualizations Created

The following visualizations were developed:

1. Monthly Revenue Trend – Analyzed revenue changes over time.
2. Top 10 Countries by Revenue – Identified major revenue-generating markets.
3. Top 10 Products by Revenue – Compared product revenue performance.
4. Top 10 Products by Quantity Sold – Identified products with the highest sales volume.
5. Weekday vs Weekend Revenue – Compared sales performance across day types.
6. Revenue by Month – Examined monthly revenue patterns.
7. Revenue Distribution – Studied the distribution of transaction-line revenue.
8. Correlation Heatmap – Examined relationships between numerical variables.
9. Daily Revenue Trend – Identified daily sales fluctuations.
10. Country-wise Revenue Contribution – Measured the contribution of leading countries.
11. Monthly Revenue Comparison by Year – Compared monthly revenue across years.
12. Sales Performance Dashboard – Combined key visualizations into one dashboard-style figure.

## 💡 Key Business Insights

- The United Kingdom was the largest revenue-generating country.
- November 2011 recorded the highest monthly revenue.
- December 2011 recorded the lowest monthly revenue.
- Product rankings differed when comparing revenue and quantity sold.
- Weekdays generated higher total recorded revenue than weekends.
- Revenue varied significantly across individual transaction lines.
- The dataset contained negative quantities and cancellation transactions that required careful interpretation.
